## 1. Executive Summary

For every Source-1 (S1) business we retrieve its Source-2/3 (S2/S3) records in three steps.

1. **Blocking.** Fourteen families of hashed blocking keys generate raw candidate pairs. A learned one-to-one cut reduces them to 6.6 candidates per S1 entity. The reduction ratio is 0.9999983, and 97.6% of true train pairs are kept.
2. **Scoring.** A gradient-boosted pair model with 76 engineered features scores every candidate pair. A fine-tuned multilingual cross-encoder (xlm-roberta-base) re-scores the 6% of pairs the pair model is unsure about.
3. **Decision.** Probabilities are recalibrated per country for label shift. A one-to-one threshold decision tuned directly for macro F0.5 then picks the matches.

France has no labels. It gets a transfer recipe chosen by leave-one-country-out validation, with self-training on its own confident pairs.

**What is in the package**
- The two files in `output/` are our best leaderboard upload, #16 (public LB 0.988708).
- That file merges this pipeline (pipeline A, §3 to §5) with pipeline B, a team member's independent pipeline (§6).
- The code in the zip regenerates pipeline A's own files byte for byte (upload #8, 0.98515).
- The zip also holds pipeline B's output file that the merge reads, in `code/business_entity_resolution/inputs/`. With it, the same command regenerates `output/` byte for byte.
- Pipeline B's code was too large to include; only its output is in the zip (§6).

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Problem Analysis

These findings from `src/eda.py` shaped the design.

**Structure of the labels (train)**
- The train set has 2,206,821 S1 entities (India 883,188; US 1,323,633), 10,320,219 S2/S3 records and 7,638,365 true pairs.
- **One-to-one.** No S2/S3 record belongs to two S1 entities (0 of 7,638,365), and no match crosses countries. The pipeline relies on both facts: blocking runs per country, and every S2/S3 record is kept under at most one S1.
- **Singletons.** 5.58% of S1 entities have no match, the same rate in both countries. An S1 has 3.46 matches on average (at most 11), and 85% have 2 to 6.
- **Distractors.** 26% of S2/S3 records belong to no S1 entity.

**Test differs from train**
- Test has 1,732,544 S1 entities: France 15.0% (with no labels at all), India 46.8%, US 38.3%.
- There are 5.75 S2/S3 records per S1 in test against 4.68 in train (+23%). Yet the model finds about as many matches per S1 as train has (3.3 against 3.46), so the extra records are distractors.
- Expectation-maximization (EM) on the model's probabilities estimates that 47% to 54% of test candidate pairs are true, against 66% in the training sample. Probabilities calibrated on train are therefore too high on test (§4.5).

**Noise patterns (train examples)**

| Field | Pattern | Example (S1 vs S2/S3) |
|---|---|---|
| Name | legal-form variants, truncation | `Rely Club Private Limited` vs `Rely Club Private` |
| Name | inserted or dropped words, honorifics | `Alive Solutions` vs `Sri Alive Solutions Company` |
| Name | word-order swaps | `Rely Club Private Limited` vs `Rely Private Club Limited` |
| Name | character typos, look-alike digits | `sai foods ltd` vs `sai foos ltd`; `br0wn` |
| Name | glued words, web names | `urban hitech systems pvt ltd` vs `urbanhitech`; `visioncenter.com` |
| Name | non-Latin scripts | Devanagari in 13.4% of S2 and 7.5% of S3 India names; other Indic scripts too |
| Address | reordering, abbreviations, spelling | `I-2/33, Block-1 Pkt-2, Rohini, Sector-16, Delhi, ...` vs `Delhi, I-2/33, BLOK-1 PKT-2, ROHINI, SECTOR-16, DELHI` |
| Address | missing parts, empty field | 2.3% to 3.7% of S2/S3 addresses are empty (S1 addresses never are) |

**Decoys.** A decoy looks like an S1 entity but is a different business:
- the same name at a different address (chains);
- the same name and street, with the house number a few doors away (+1 to +21), a pattern a teammate spotted;
- a different legal form (`Pvt Ltd` against `LLP` is a different company);
- a different trade name at a shared address (malls, office buildings).

**Metric implications.** F0.5 weighs precision more than recall, and every entity counts equally. A correctly empty prediction scores 1.0; a wrong match on a singleton scores 0. So an uncertain candidate should be left out, and the decision layer optimizes macro F0.5 directly.

### 2.2 Solution Strategy

**Approach Type:** Hybrid. Pipeline A is blocking, then a gradient-boosted classifier, then a transformer cross-encoder on the uncertain pairs (two-stage scoring), then a one-to-one decision. The packaged file then merges pipeline A with a second, independent pipeline (§6).

**Core Innovation:**
1. **A learned one-to-one cut.**
   - Each S2/S3 record belongs to at most one S1, so a record is kept only under its best S1 and close runners-up, instead of keeping a fixed top-K of records per S1.
   - The runners-up are ranked by a small cross-fitted model on cheap similarities and on which blocking keys fired.
   - On train, 63 raw pairs per S1 become 5.1, at pair completeness 0.976.
2. **Learned "noise or decoy?" word scores.**
   - These are out-of-fold, typo-aware target encodings of the words by which two names differ.
   - An extra `Sri` or `Services` is harmless; an extra `Exports` or `LLP` usually marks another company.
3. **A recipe for a country without labels, chosen by leave-one-country-out** (train on one country, score the other).
   - The word scores are re-learned from that country's own confident pairs (self-training).
   - The cross-encoder is adapted with pseudo-labeled pairs of that country.
   - The label shift is corrected per country with EM.
4. **A cross-encoder only where it matters.**
   - xlm-roberta-base reads the raw text of the ~6% of pairs the pair model is unsure about.
   - A stage-2 model combines both opinions with competition features.
   - Out-of-fold F0.5 rises from 0.9838 to 0.9874.

```
dataset/*.tsv
 -> normalization + learned token maps               prepare.py, normalize.py
 -> 14 hashed blocking key families, per country      blocking.py                  67.2 raw pairs per test S1
 -> cheap similarities + learned one-to-one cut       candidates.py, cut_model.py  6.60 pairs per S1
 -> pair model: HistGradientBoosting, 76 features     features.py, te.py, train.py, predict.py
 -> hard pairs (0.02 <= p <= 0.98): cross-encoder     colab/*.ipynb (GPU)          ~6% of the pairs
 -> stage 2: pair model + cross-encoder               stage2.py
 -> EM prior shift per country, one-to-one decision at 0.725              decide.py        (pipeline A = upload #8)
 -> team merge with pipeline B (§6)                   team_merge.py
 -> output/matching_results.tsv, output/candidate_pairs.tsv               validator: PASS  (upload #16)
```

## 3. Candidate Generation (Blocking)

Blocking runs separately for each country, in three steps:
1. The union of 14 hashed key families gives the **raw pairs**.
2. Five cheap similarities are computed for every raw pair.
3. A learned one-to-one cut keeps the **final candidates**.

The final candidates are exactly the pairs the models score and the pairs written to `candidate_pairs.tsv`.

### 3.1 Normalization (`normalize.py`, `prepare.py`)

Blocking and the features share this normalization.

**Script and case**
- Non-ASCII text (accents, Devanagari and other Indic scripts) is transliterated with `anyascii` (ISC license).
- Everything is lowercased, and `&` becomes `and`.
- In names, dots are removed (`L.L.P.` becomes `llp`); in addresses, dots become spaces (`No.74` becomes `no 74`). Other punctuation becomes spaces.

**Names**
- Web suffixes are stripped (`www.`, `.com`, `.co.in`, `.fr`, ...).
- In tokens that mix letters and digits, look-alike digits are fixed: `br0wn` becomes `brown`, `materia1s` becomes `materials`.
- Legal forms are canonicalized: `private` and `pte` become `pvt`, `limited` becomes `ltd`, `corporation` becomes `corp`, `incorporated` becomes `inc`, and `et` and `n` become `and`.
- French legal forms (`sarl`, `sas`, `sasu`, `eurl`, `sa`, `sci`, `snc`, `scop`) are kept as tokens.
- Phone-like numbers (6 or more digits) and immediately repeated words are dropped.
- A **core name** also removes legal forms, connectors, honorifics (`sri`, `shri`, `smt`, `dr`, ...), web tokens and `dba`.

**Addresses**
- Street types and common words are canonicalized in English and French alike:
  - `street` becomes `st`, `road` becomes `rd`, `avenue` and `av` become `ave`, `boulevard` and `bd` become `blvd`;
  - `r` becomes `rue`, `chemin` becomes `chem`;
  - `nagar` becomes `ngr`, `sector` becomes `sec`;
  - `nr` becomes `near`, and `opposite` becomes `opp`.
- Number sequences (house, unit and postal numbers) are extracted.

**Token maps learned from the training labels**
- Some matched train pairs have names or addresses that differ by exactly one word. These pairs give frequent, dominant substitutions, always from the S2/S3 spelling to the S1 spelling:
  - 684 word maps for names written in a non-Latin script;
  - 12,650 name-word substitutions: typos and variants that never occur in S1, such as `privte` to `pvt` and `lndia` to `india`;
  - 1,815 address-word substitutions, such as `mh` and `mharastr` to `maharashtra`.
- The maps are applied to all records of all countries. They use only the provided training labels.

### 3.2 Blocking keys (`blocking.py`)

**Raw pairs.** Each record emits keys from its normalized fields. An S1 record and an S2/S3 record that share a key form a raw pair.

**Rare tokens.** A token's rarity is its document frequency among the **S1** records of the country. A token that never occurs in S1, such as the typo `wirless`, can never produce a match, so it never takes a key slot. Each record uses:
- its 3 rarest name tokens (at least 2 characters);
- its 2 rarest address tokens (at least 3 characters, no digits);
- its first 2 distinct numbers. The `cn`, `sn`, `acr` and `nn` keys use the first 4.

Keys are 64-bit hashes, with one seed per family.

**Block-size caps.** A key is dropped when its block holds more than `max S1` S1 records or more than `max pairs` pairs. Without the caps, chains, generic words and malls would create huge blocks. With them, every block is bounded, and the number of raw pairs grows linearly with the number of keys. Nothing is compared all-pairs.

| Family | Key | Targets | Caps (max S1 / max pairs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nt` | rare name token × address number | typos elsewhere, reordered words | 20 / 600 |
| `na` | rare name token × rare address token | house number missing or mistyped | 20 / 600 |
| `an` | address number × rare address token | heavily corrupted name | 10 / 200 |
| `tt` | every pair of the 3 rarest name tokens | empty or different address | 20 / 400 |
| `ttn` | pair of rare name tokens × house number | common words, typo in the rest | 20 / 600 |
| `t1` | a single rare name token (each of the 2 rarest) | typo in the other word, no address | 5 / 100 |
| `cat` | core name glued (spaces and glued legal words removed) | `ridgeinitiative.com`, `bababusinessprivate` | 20 / 400 |
| `cats` | core words sorted, then glued | reordered names | 20 / 400 |
| `cn` | glued core name × house number | common name, short address | 20 / 600 |
| `sn` | sorted glued core name × house number | reordered common names | 20 / 600 |
| `ca` | glued core name × any address word | `sky marketing` + `chennai` | 20 / 600 |
| `acr` | S1 initials × number, against a short glued record name × number | acronyms (`Universal Foods` = `UF`) | 10 / 200 |
| `nn` | pair of house numbers | a different trade name at the same detailed address | 10 / 200 |
| `aa` | pair of the 3 rarest address tokens | same address without house numbers | 10 / 200 |

All frequencies are computed on the data being blocked. Nothing is fitted to a particular country, and France is blocked by the same code. Appendix B gives the pair count of each family.

### 3.3 Cheap scores and the learned one-to-one cut (`candidates.py`, `cut_model.py`)

**Cheap scores.** Every raw pair gets five cheap similarities: rapidfuzz scores from 0 to 100, plus an acronym flag.
- token-set ratio of the core names;
- ratio of the glued core names;
- token-set ratio of the addresses;
- token-set ratio of the address numbers;
- acronym flag.

**Cut model**
- Its inputs are these five similarities plus 14 blocking-family flags.
- It is a small HistGradientBoosting classifier (200 trees, 31 leaves) that outputs a ranking logit.
- It is fitted on 6M sampled raw train pairs, with the label "true pair".
- It is **cross-fitted** by S1 parity: pairs of even-id S1 entities are scored by the model fitted on odd ones, and the reverse. So the cut on train never uses a pair's own label.
- Test pairs get the mean logit of both models.

**Rule.** Every S2/S3 record keeps its best S1, plus the second and third best if they are within 2.0 logits of it (`m_max 3`, `delta 2.0`). This is the smallest candidate set within 0.0005 of the best pooled pair completeness (PC) on this train curve:

| Cut rule | India PC | India cand/S1 | US PC | US cand/S1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no cut (raw blocking) | 0.9748 | 68.2 | 0.9827 | 59.8 |
| hand-weighted score (name + 0.5 address + 0.25 number), best 3 within 10 | 0.9639 | 5.57 | 0.9766 | 5.11 |
| learned, best 2 within 1.0 | 0.9691 | 4.88 | 0.9759 | 4.79 |
| learned, best 3 within 1.5 | 0.9712 | 5.13 | 0.9782 | 4.95 |
| **learned, best 3 within 2.0 (used)** | **0.9716** | **5.28** | **0.9785** | **5.05** |
| learned, best 3 within 2.5 | 0.9719 | 5.42 | 0.9787 | 5.14 |

The cut loses 0.3% to 0.4% of true pairs while removing 92% of the raw pairs. A per-S1 top-5 of about the same size kept only 0.82 to 0.85 of the true pairs (measured on an earlier blocking version); the one-to-one structure avoids that loss.

- 572 test S1 entities (0.03%) have no candidate.

- **Pair completeness** is the share of true pairs among the candidates. Test has no labels, so it is measured on all train entities.

### 3.5 How we ensured true matches were not lost

**Measured, not assumed**
- Every blocking change was judged on the full train set by three numbers: pair completeness, candidates per S1, and the oracle F0.5 (the F0.5 a perfect model would reach on the candidates).
- Over five blocking versions, completeness rose from 0.80 (India) and 0.90 (US) at 16 to 19 candidates per S1 (v1) to 0.972 and 0.979 at 5.1 to 5.3.

**Complementary keys.** Each family targets a failure mode found among the missed pairs of the previous version:
- typos: `nt`, `ttn`, `t1`;
- word order: `cats`, `sn`;
- glued names: `cat`;
- acronyms: `acr`;
- corrupted names at the same address: `an`, `nn`, `aa`;
- empty addresses: `tt`, `t1`.

Rare tokens are ranked by S1 frequency, so typos never use up key slots.

**One-to-one instead of top-K.** The learned cut keeps each record's plausible owners (up to 3), not the top K records of each S1.

**What remains missed (train, share of all true pairs)**
- Blocking misses 2.52% of true pairs in India and 1.73% in US. The cut loses another 0.31% and 0.42%.
- The largest missed group is S2/S3 records with an empty address whose name many S1 entities share (median 50 in India, 27 in US). They are 1.23% (India) and 1.31% (US) of true pairs.
- A name-only key would put them in blocks of 27 to 50 S1 entities. Even a perfect model could not tell which same-name business an address-less record belongs to, so the caps drop these blocks on purpose.

## 4. Matching Model

The model has two stages:
- **Stage 1** is a gradient-boosted pair classifier on engineered features. It scores all 11.4M pairs.
- **Stage 2** covers the pairs stage 1 is unsure about. A cross-encoder reads their raw text, and a second small gradient-boosted model combines both opinions.

### 4.1 Features used (stage 1: 76 features; `features.py`, `te.py`)

No feature identifies the country. Every frequency statistic is computed within the country being scored.

| Group | # | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Name similarity | 5 | rapidfuzz `ratio` on full normalized names; `ratio`, `token_sort_ratio`, `partial_ratio` and Jaro-Winkler on core names |
| Address similarity | 2 | `ratio`, `partial_ratio` on normalized addresses |
| Cheap scores | 6 | the five cut inputs (core-name token set, glued-name ratio, address token set, number token set, acronym) and the learned cut logit |
| House numbers | 11 | common numbers, count per side, Jaccard, first numbers equal, S1's first number anywhere in the record and the reverse, conflict (both have numbers, none shared), edit similarity of the first numbers, **`num_first_diff`, `num_min_shift`** |
| Legal form | 4 | same; partial (`Ltd` vs `Pvt Ltd`); conflict (`Pvt Ltd` vs `LLP`); missing on one side |
| Word-difference scores | 11 | "noise or decoy?" scores of extra and missing words (min, mean, max, count, unseen count per side), `n_typo` |
| Context and frequency | 14 | S1 entities sharing each side's core name (chains) and exact address (malls); S2/S3 records carrying each side's core name; token counts; empty-address flags; non-Latin origin; digits in name; S2 or S3 |
| Competition | 8 | count, rank, gap to the best and margin over the best *other* candidate, within the S1's raw candidates and within the record's raw candidates |
| Blocking provenance | 15 | which of the 14 families produced the pair, and how many |

**House-number shift**
- `num_first_diff` is the record's first number minus the S1's first number.
- `num_min_shift` is the smallest non-zero difference over all pairs of their numbers.
- A look-alike decoy keeps the name and street but sits a few doors away (+1 to +21). Noise changes digits instead (`1370` vs `137`).
- A teammate's observation led to these two features. They added +0.0015 CV and +0.0065 in leave-one-country-out.

**Word-difference scores (`te.py`)**
1. For each pair we list the words found only in the S2/S3 name (*extra*) and only in the S1 name (*missing*).
2. An extra and a missing word that are nearly the same string (ratio ≥ 80, or one edit on words of 3+ letters) are one typo. They are removed from both lists and counted in `n_typo`.
3. For every word and side, a smoothed log-odds of "the pair is a match" is estimated from the training labels, relative to the base rate. The smoothing pseudo-count is 20, and the estimate is **out-of-fold**, so a training row never sees its own label.
4. A word never seen in training scores 0 (neutral).

These scores were introduced in v3, together with a new blocking version; CV moved from 0.9646 to 0.9794.

### 4.2 Model type (stage 1)

- **Model.** scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingClassifier`: learning rate 0.1, 63 leaves, at least 50 samples per leaf, L2 1.0, up to 1,500 trees with early stopping (10% validation, patience 30).
- **Training data.** The candidates of a deterministic 25% of train S1 entities: 550,877 entities and 2,832,478 pairs, 65.7% of them positive.
- **Folds.** The 4 fold models used 1,077 to 1,500 trees. Test probabilities are their mean.
- **Cost.** Training takes 51 min on the 8 GB M2, including the leave-one-country-out runs, with a peak memory of 8.2 GB.

### 4.3 Cross-encoder (`ce_export.py`, `colab/*.ipynb`)

**Hard pairs.** A pair is hard when its stage-1 probability is in [0.02, 0.98].
- In the validation sample, 5.8% of the pairs are hard (164,745 pairs, 51% of them true).
- On test there are 263,660 hard pairs in India, 227,700 in US and 183,587 in France.

**Model.** `FacebookAI/xlm-roberta-base` (MIT license, 278M parameters) is used as a pair classifier with one output logit.
- The input is `name | address` of the S1 record and of the S2/S3 record, as **raw text**. It reads Devanagari and French accents directly and is independent of our normalization.
- Training settings: truncation to 96 tokens, BCE loss, AdamW, fp16, batch 32, one epoch per round, linear schedule. It was trained on free Google Colab T4 GPUs.

**No leakage.** A hash splits the train S1 entities into three disjoint groups:
- 25% for the pair model and stage 2 (the validation sample);
- 30% for cross-encoder round 1;
- 45% for round 2.

So the cross-encoder never sees the label of a validation entity.

| Round | Data | Learning rate | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 357,771 pairs of the 30% group: all hard pairs + 3% of the easy ones | 2e-5 | hard-pair AUC 0.960, against 0.923 for the pair model (held-out 2%) |
| 2 | continues round 1 on 510,000 pairs: 450,000 of the 45% group + 60,000 French test pairs pseudo-labeled by the France model (p ≤ 0.03 → 0, p ≥ 0.97 → 1) | 1e-5 | hard-pair AUC 0.948 → 0.957, log loss 0.284 → 0.257 (same held-out pairs) |
| 3 (countries without labels) | continues round 2 on 150,000 pseudo-labeled French pairs + 50,000 labeled train pairs | 1e-5 | rescores France's hard pairs |

The pseudo-labels come from our own model's probabilities (self-training). No pair is ever labeled by hand.

### 4.4 Stage 2 (`stage2.py`)

**Model.** A small `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` (up to 400 trees, learning rate 0.05, 15 leaves, at least 100 samples per leaf).

**Features.** Six per hard pair:
- the stage-1 logit;
- the cross-encoder logit;
- its gap to the best cross-encoder logit among the same S1's hard pairs;
- its margin over the best logit of the same S2/S3 record under another S1;
- the number of hard pairs of the S1;
- the number of hard pairs of the record.

**Training.** Out-of-fold on the 164,745 hard pairs of the validation sample, with the same S1-grouped folds. Pairs that are not hard keep their stage-1 probability.

| AUC on the validation sample's hard pairs | |
|---|---|
| stage 1 (pair model) | 0.888 |
| cross-encoder (round 2) | 0.930 |
| **stage 2** | **0.960** |

Out-of-fold macro F0.5 rises from 0.9838 (stage 1) to 0.9874 (stage 2).

### 4.5 Decision layer, threshold and singletons (`decide.py`)

1. **Label-shift correction.**
   - Test holds more distractors than train. For each test country, the share of true pairs among the candidates is therefore re-estimated with EM on the model's own probabilities (Saerens, Latinne & Decaestecker, 2002). No labels are needed.
   - The probabilities are then recalibrated to that share: France 0.475, India 0.493, US 0.544, against 0.657 in training.
   - On the public leaderboard, this beat the plain threshold (0.958 against 0.957).
2. **One-to-one.** Every S2/S3 record is kept only under its most probable S1.
3. **Threshold selection method.**
   - A pair is kept when its recalibrated probability is at least **0.725**.
   - This threshold maximizes out-of-fold macro F0.5, computed exactly as the leaderboard defines it. The search grid is 0.20 to 0.95 in steps of 0.025, plus 0.96 to 0.99. The optimum is flat: 0.9838 at 0.70, 0.9836 at 0.80 (stage 1).
   - For countries without labels, leave-one-country-out chose the threshold, and it also gave 0.725.
4. **Singletons.** An S1 with no pair above the threshold is predicted empty.
   - That is 5.9% of test S1 entities for pipeline A (5.7% in the packaged merge), close to the 5.6% train singleton rate.
   - On validation, 1,375 of the 550,877 entities (0.25%) are wrongly empty, and 183 true singletons (0.03%) get a match.
   - Two alternatives were tested and not used. A rule that fills empty predictions lowered CV. A per-entity expected-F0.5 top-k decision scored 0.9871, against 0.9873 for the threshold.

### 4.6 France: a country without training labels

**Definition.** A country without training data is any test country absent from train. No country name appears in the code, and nothing assumes French.

**Leave-one-country-out (LOCO)** simulates this case. We train on one of India and US, score the other, and compare three variants for the unseen country:

| Unseen-country variant (stage 1, threshold 0.725) | India held out | US held out | Mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| word scores from the training countries | 0.9275 | 0.9486 | 0.9380 |
| no word scores | 0.9435 | 0.9713 | 0.9574 |
| **self-trained word scores (chosen)** | **0.9576** | 0.9700 | **0.9638** |

**Self-trained word scores**
1. A model without word scores rates the unseen country's candidate pairs.
2. The pairs with p ≤ 0.03 or p ≥ 0.97 act as labels for that country's own word statistics, out-of-fold.
3. The full model then scores the country.

This way, words that distinguish French businesses get real scores instead of looking "unseen". In LOCO, 98% to 99% of these pseudo-labels were correct.

**The rest of the France pipeline**
- Cross-encoder rounds 2 and 3 are fine-tuned on French pseudo-labeled pairs.
- Stage 2 is applied with France's own stage-1 logit.
- France gets its own EM prior shift.

Refitting the whole model on pseudo-labels scored 0.9562 in the same check, against 0.9573 for self-trained word scores, so it is not used.

**Measured effect.** France can only be scored on the public leaderboard. We estimate it as (LB − 0.85 × India/US CV) / 0.15. By that estimate, France rose from about 0.92 (v3) to about 0.973 (v6, pipeline A's final version).

### 4.7 Validation scheme

- **Sample and folds.** A 25% sample of train S1 entities is drawn by hashing the entity id. Folds are `hash(S1 id) mod 4`, so all candidates of an entity share a fold (grouped folds). Everything downstream (word scores, stage 2, thresholds) is out-of-fold on these folds.
- **Scored exactly like the leaderboard.** Macro F0.5 is computed over all sampled S1 entities, including those without candidates, which are predicted empty; blocking losses therefore count. The implementation (`metrics.py`) passes the 0.714 unit test of the problem statement.
- **Test-mix weighting.** Per-country scores are also reported in the test set's India/US mix (55/45).
- **LOCO for France**, as above.
- **Trust in CV.** The LOCO-based leaderboard estimates matched the public leaderboard (v3: estimate 0.9693, LB 0.970). CV was therefore trusted over small leaderboard changes.

## 5. Results & Error Analysis

**F0.5 Score (macro)**
- Out-of-fold on 550,877 train S1 entities: **0.9874** (India 0.9870, US 0.9876); **0.9873** in the test country mix.
- Public leaderboard: 0.98515 for pipeline A alone (upload #8); **0.988708** for the packaged team merge (upload #16, §6).

**Where the remaining F0.5 goes** (final model, out-of-fold)

Pair-level precision is 0.9983 and recall is 0.9644, over 1.9M true pairs. The oracle F0.5 on the candidates (a perfect model) is 0.9924.

| Cause (per S1 entity) | Share of entities | F0.5 lost |
|---|---|---|
| true matches never became candidates (blocking or cut) | 7.3% | 0.0058 |
| true matches among the candidates, not matched | 3.3% | 0.0025 |
| entity has matches but was predicted empty | 0.25% | 0.0025 |
| false matches only | 0.46% | 0.0010 |
| mixed (false and missed matches) | 0.27% | 0.0005 |
| true singleton given a match | 0.03% | 0.0003 |
| perfect | 88.4% | 0 |

**Counterfactual gains**
- **+0.0078** if blocking lost nothing;
- **+0.0036** if every candidate match were found;
- **+0.0015** if no false match were made.

Recall, above all blocking recall, is the remaining lever. Precision is already near its ceiling.

**Common false positives (wrong merges).** The validation sample has 3,140 false pairs, 0.17% of the predicted pairs. The kinds below are assigned in the order listed, and each pair gets the first kind that fits.
1. **Legal-form conflict** (1%).
2. **Look-alike a few doors away** (7%): the house numbers conflict, and the closest two differ by 25 or less.
3. **House numbers disagree** (15%).
4. **Record without address** (28%): the record carries the S1's name. For 54% of these false matches, other S1 entities share that name.
5. **Shared address** (7%): a different business name at an address several S1 entities share.
6. **Chain name** (23%): several S1 entities share the name.
7. **Names differ by extra or missing words** (14%).
8. **Other** (6%).

Typical examples (S1 against record):
- `alvarez dynamix` against `alvarez dynamix` with no address;
- `anchor llc`, `600 stewarts ferry pike`, against `anchor llc`, `3896 stewarts ln`;
- `prime defense solutions`, `6149 258th st`, against `prime defense solutions llc`, `6148 258th st`;
- `falcon`, `5540 arthur st`, against `falcon corp`, `554 arthur st`;
- `meeks platinum llc` against `abreliablelloyds` in the same unit.

Most of these are ambiguous even for a human: a record with no address and a name several businesses share, or a one-digit difference that may be a typo or a neighbour.

**Common false negatives (missed matches)**

*Missed by blocking* (shares of all true pairs, India / US):
- **An S2/S3 record with an empty address whose name many S1 entities share** (1.23% / 1.31%). These are the capped blocks of §3.5, such as `one consultancy`, which 52 S1 entities share.
- **A different trade name, or a glued or abbreviated form, with only a number shared** (0.56% / 0.14%). Examples:
  - `international urban exports ltd` against `eiurban`;
  - `chennai city corp producer ltd` against `cityproducerchennai`;
  - `home ventures` against `orbilyralum` at the same address.
- **A common name plus a typo and a changed number** (0.42% / 0.03%). Example: `sun tech pvt ltd`, `17 1 bharat apartments`, against `sun tech pirighle ltd`, `7 1 bharat apartments`, where 60 S1 entities share `sun tech`.

*Missed among the candidates.* The validation sample has 21,265 such true pairs.
- **73% have an empty record address.** The name matches (median core-name ratio 100), and for 93% of them other S1 entities share the S1's name. The model gives about 0.46, below the threshold, which is the F0.5-optimal choice under that uncertainty.
- The rest, under the same kinds as the false positives:
  - heavily changed names (6%);
  - chain names (6%);
  - look-alike numbers (5%);
  - true pairs whose house numbers disagree (5%);
  - shared addresses (4%);
  - legal-form conflicts (2%).

**What did not help**

| Tried | Result |
|---|---|
| stage 2 with both cross-encoder rounds | CV 0.9873, unchanged |
| raw pair features in stage 2 | CV 0.9872 |
| expected-F0.5 decision | CV 0.9871 |
| rule filling empty predictions | lowered CV |
| model self-training for France | −0.001 in LOCO |
| xlm-roberta-large cross-encoder (MIT, 560M; `kaggle/`) as an extra stage-2 input | hard-pair AUC 0.9603 → 0.9617, CV 0.9873 unchanged |
| xlm-roberta-large instead of round 2 | CV 0.9872 |
| in the team merge (§6), pipeline A's confident French matches added to pipeline B's | public LB 0.986333, against 0.986375 without them |

The packaged `matching_results.tsv` is leaderboard upload #16, our best (public LB 0.988708). It merges two pipelines built on the same provided files:
- **pipeline A**: this document, §3 to §5;
- **pipeline B**: the independent pipeline of team member Kartikey Kashyap.

**Pipeline B, as reported by its author**

| | Version 1 | Latest version |
|---|---|---|
| Used for | uploads #9 to #13, and France in #15 | the packaged file: India/US votes and France |
| Cross-encoder | xlm-roberta-base (MIT, 278M parameters), run with JAX on a TPU | xlm-roberta-base and xlm-roberta-large (MIT, 560M parameters) |
| Macro F0.5 on held-out train entities | 0.9900 (221,114 entities, all train records competing) | 0.99066 |
| Public leaderboard, alone | 0.98542 | not scored |

- Both versions have their own blocking (about 51 candidates per S1, the figure reported for a later version), pair features and a gradient-boosted model.
- The latest file changes only the French matches of the version before it: 880,078 pairs instead of 868,793 (version 1 has 890,613). Its India/US matches, and therefore its held-out score, are the same.

**Pipeline B's file in the zip:** `code/business_entity_resolution/inputs/pipeline_b_latest_matching_results.tsv`, the latest version's `matching_results.tsv` for every test S1 entity (md5 a806a079608d95db7f49cfb036e319dd).

**What the zip regenerates**
- `src/run_pipeline.py` regenerates pipeline A's files byte for byte (upload #8, checked with md5).
- `src/run_pipeline.py --mate inputs/pipeline_b_latest_matching_results.tsv` adds the merge. It regenerates `output/` byte for byte (upload #16, checked with md5).
- Pipeline B's code, with its model files, was too large to include. The merge needs only its output file above.

`src/team_merge.py` needs only pipeline A's final test probabilities and pipeline B's matches. It uses no labels and adds no pair by hand.

**Merge rule**
- **India and US**
  - Each pipeline-A candidate pair's posterior odds are A's odds × 8 if B matched the pair, and ÷ 8 if it did not. B's decision is evidence of fixed strength: where A is confident, A decides; near the threshold, B decides.
  - The 42,615 pairs that B matched but A never generated get probability 0.9. Examples are acronyms, glued names and typos that A's blocking misses.
  - The usual one-to-one decision at 0.725 follows.
- **Countries without training data (France):** B's matches are kept as they are.

**Chosen on the public leaderboard.** It is the only feedback available for France, so these settings are tuned to it:

| File | Public LB |
|---|---|
| pipeline A alone (upload #8) | 0.98515 |
| pipeline B version 1 alone (vote strength ∞) | 0.98542 |
| merge with B version 1, vote × 1.5 | 0.986093 |
| merge with B version 1, vote × 3 | 0.986375 |
| merge with B version 1, vote × 8 (upload #13) | 0.986435 |
| merge × 3 with A's France (B's vote × 3 there too) | 0.986333 |
| merge × 8 with a later B version, its France included (upload #14) | below 0.986435 (exact score not recorded) |
| merge × 8: a later B version for India/US, B version 1's France (upload #15) | 0.98682 |
| **merge × 8: B's latest version, its France included (packaged, upload #16)** | **0.988708** |

- The vote-strength curve is flat between × 3 and × 8. The gain over either pipeline alone (+0.0010 to +0.0013) comes from their disagreements, not from one particular setting.
- Uploads #15 and #16 differ only in France, on 34,620 French entities (2.0% of test S1). The gain of +0.0019 (0.98682 to 0.988708) therefore comes from B's latest French matches. They leave 5.56% of French entities empty, close to the no-match rate of the training countries (5.58%), against 5.39% for version 1.
- Pipeline A was not used to settle French pairs. It gives a probability below 0.02 to 1.5% of B's latest French matches, against 0.04% to 0.07% of its India/US matches. Its low French scores are therefore not reliable enough for that.

**Candidate set of the packaged file**
- `candidate_pairs.tsv` holds every pair the merge decision ranks: 11,486,821 pairs, 6.63 per S1. They are:
  - pipeline A's 11,428,674 candidates;
  - the 42,615 India/US pairs that B matched outside them;
  - the 15,532 French pairs that B matched outside them.
- Every match is a candidate. The code asserts this, and the validator passes.
- **Disclosure:** pipeline B's own model scored its own, larger candidate set (about 51 per S1, as reported by its author) before making its decisions. Only its final matches enter the merge.

---

## 7. Conclusion

A recall-oriented but strictly bounded blocking stage, cut by a learned one-to-one rule to 6.6 candidates per entity, followed by a strong gradient-boosted pair model, reaches 0.984 CV. A multilingual cross-encoder spent only on the uncertain 6% of pairs adds another +0.0036. Our main lessons:
1. **Exploit the structure of the data.** The one-to-one constraint more than halved the candidate set at higher recall, and it is also the backbone of the decision.
2. **Learn what a word difference means** instead of hand-listing stop words.
3. **For an unseen country, choose the transfer recipe by leave-one-country-out.** Some of our strongest train features hurt France until they were re-learned from France's own confident pairs.

### C. Rule Compliance

- **Data.** Only the provided files are used: no external data, APIs, geocoding, registries or web lookups.
- **Unlabeled test files.** They are used only in unsupervised ways: frequency statistics, the EM prior shift, and pseudo-labels produced by our own model for the country without labels.
- **Labels and language models.** No pair was labeled by hand, and no large language model is called anywhere in the pipeline.
- **Models.** `xlm-roberta-base` is MIT-licensed with 278M parameters, well under 8B; the rest is gradient boosting.
- **Countries.** No country list and no country feature. The behaviour for countries without labels follows from "test country absent from train".
- **Scaling.** Blocking is hash-based with block-size caps, so nothing is compared all-pairs, not even within a country. The raw-to-final filtering is a cheap learned cut, documented in §3.3.
- **Pipeline B (§6).** As reported by its author, it uses only the provided files. Its version 1 uses xlm-roberta-base (MIT, 278M parameters). Its later versions, including the one behind the packaged file, add xlm-roberta-large (MIT, 560M parameters). Its code is not in this zip; its output file is (§6).
